The thesis

The best AI infrastructure markets are the ones no one is fighting over.

Northern Virginia, Phoenix, and Dallas are saturated. Power is expensive. Land is expensive. Competition is fierce. Every hyperscaler is building there because everyone else is building there.

Secondary markets — mid-size cities and industrial corridors with natural gas infrastructure, available land, and an established industrial workforce — offer the same connectivity at a fraction of the cost. And nobody is building there at scale.

Dominion's thesis: build AI data centers in secondary markets where the power cost advantage is structural, the competitive landscape is open, and demand from regional enterprises, research institutions, and government agencies is real and growing. Operate them. Scale the portfolio.

Competitive advantages

Two structural edges.
Both are durable.

Cheap power gets attention. But the more interesting advantage is what happens to Dominion's business model when AI demand contracts.

Advantage 01 — Power
$0.02/kWh.
They pay $0.10.

On-site natural gas generation eliminates grid dependency for the primary compute load. At full 25 MW utilization, Dominion's power cost advantage over a grid-dependent operator is approximately $7M per year.

This isn't an arbitrage that closes. Natural gas pipeline access is a physical asset tied to the site. The savings compound with every megawatt of capacity that comes online.

It means Dominion can price compute services materially below cloud competitors and still generate strong gross margins — and the economics work at lower utilization levels that would be loss-making for a grid-dependent operator.

Advantage 02 — Resilience
Built to survive
an AI bust.

The fiber overbuild of the late 90s is the reference case. Pure-play GPU rental companies — high fixed costs, limited revenue diversification, dependent on sustained AI demand — are the most exposed when enterprise adoption contracts.

Dominion's campus generates revenue from four streams: compute services, the fiber hub, the training center, and co-working. The fiber hub is recession-proof. The training center runs on employer contracts, not hype cycles.

And at $0.02/kWh, Dominion reaches break-even at a materially lower utilization rate than any grid-dependent competitor. A contraction that pushes a CoreWeave-equivalent into distress leaves Dominion operating at reduced — but positive — margin.

Merlyn AI Labs — the sister company built by the same team — provides the anchor tenant demand that reinforces this further. Their revenue doesn't depend on AI being a hot investment theme. When speculative compute demand contracts elsewhere, the campus stays utilized. That's not a coincidence. It's the design.
