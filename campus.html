Campus operating models

Built to last.
Three ways to finish.

The Civic AI Campus is Dominion's model for building hyperscale AI data centers in secondary markets and transferring ownership to the communities that need them most — through a structured installment sale to a locally governed Public Benefit Corporation. A hyperscale AI data center, a workforce training center, a regional fiber hub, and co-working space — under one roof, owned by the community.

Three paths to maturity

What's on the campus

Four components.
One integrated facility.

The campus components are not co-located by coincidence. They share power infrastructure, networking, security, and staffing in ways that reduce cost and increase resilience for each one.

01
The data center
A 6–25 MW AI compute facility built to Dominion's institutional standard — NVIDIA and AMD GPU infrastructure, on-site natural gas generation at $0.02/kWh, tiered storage, and a multi-tenant control plane. Not a cut-down community version. The same hardware that serves regional enterprises.
02
The training center
5,000–10,000 sq ft of learning space for workforce training — corporate contracts, public workshops, and GPU lab sessions. The training center uses the same compute cluster the enterprise clients do, with a sandboxed partition for educational workloads.
03
The co-working space
3,000–5,000 sq ft for local startups, remote workers, and early-stage companies who benefit from proximity to the compute infrastructure. Modest recurring revenue and meaningful foot traffic during off-peak hours.
04
The fiber hub
The campus serves as a regional fiber aggregation point — connecting to major backbone providers and extending last-mile fiber to underserved areas in the county. Predictable recurring revenue and a piece of public communications infrastructure that strengthens the case for federal financing.

Path C — Community transfer

How the installment
sale works.

In markets where community transfer is the right outcome, Dominion facilitates a structured installment sale to a locally governed Public Benefit Corporation. Title transfers at closing. The community pays from campus operating revenue over 15–25 years.

1
Site identification and community engagement
Dominion screens markets for natural gas access, land availability, employer demand, and civic institutional will. A community sponsor — chamber, economic development authority, or community foundation — co-sponsors the B Corp formation.
Months 1–3
2
B Corp formation and community share offering
The community Public Benefit Corporation is formed with steward-ownership provisions. A Regulation CF offering opens community shares at $500–$1,000 minimums — converting subscribers into institutional stakeholders, not just customers.
Months 2–6
3
Campus build and operation
Dominion constructs and operates the campus under a management agreement with the B Corp. Revenue builds from managed IT services, Academy training, compute services, and fiber connectivity. Operating history is established.
Months 9–36
4
USDA Community Facilities financing
The campus qualifies for USDA Community Facilities program loans covering 40–60% of the purchase price. The fiber hub and training center anchor the application. The 12–18 month approval cycle is filed in Month 4 of Year 1 so it closes on schedule.
Pre-application Month 4 · approval Year 2–3
5
Installment sale closes
The B Corp purchases the campus from Dominion. Title transfers at closing. USDA financing covers the majority. Remaining balance is seller-financed by Dominion at 3–5% over 15–20 years — fully amortizing, no balloon. The community owns the infrastructure outright at maturity.
Year 3–4

Federal and state financing

Financing structures
commercial operators can't use.

The community ownership model unlocks capital instruments that are unavailable to commercial data center developers. The integrated campus structure — fiber hub + training center + compute — makes every one of these programs applicable.

USDA
Community Facilities
Direct loans and grants for essential community facilities. The fiber hub and training center anchor the application. Covers 40–60% of campus acquisition price.
NTIA
BEAD Broadband
Infrastructure funding covering the fiber hub buildout and last-mile extension to underserved areas in the county.
EDA
Economic Adjustment
Construction and equipment grants for campuses in communities with documented economic decline. Applies to most secondary markets in the Midwest.
NMTC
New Markets Tax Credits
For campuses in qualifying low-income census tracts, provides an effective subsidy of 15–20% on qualified project costs.
CDFI
Impact Lending
Community Development Financial Institutions provide below-market capital for community development purposes. Covers an additional 15–25% of the acquisition price.
Reg CF
Community Shares
Community members subscribe for $500–$1,000. Not a financial investment — a civic membership. Converts the community into institutional stakeholders before the campus opens.
