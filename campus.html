The campus strategy

One facility.
Three ways to finish.

Every Dominion campus is an integrated AI infrastructure facility — data center, training center, fiber hub, and co-working space — built to institutional standards. What happens at maturity is not predetermined. Which of three paths makes sense depends on the market, the investors, and the community.

Three paths to maturity What gets built

Long-term operating models

Three paths.
Each one is a real outcome.

These aren't theoretical options. Each path has a clear trigger, a clear financial profile, and a clear set of stakeholders who benefit. Which one Dominion pursues at any given campus depends on what the market and the investors want at maturity.

Path A
Hold and operate

Dominion retains ownership and continues operating the campus indefinitely. Each new campus improves the economics of the existing network — utilization arbitrage, shared tooling, compounding operational expertise.

At full buildout across three sites — Indiana, Mississippi, Nevada — the portfolio generates approximately $1.1B in annual EBITDA at 144 MW combined. At that scale the platform becomes institutional-grade infrastructure: large enough for IPO consideration, attractive to sovereign wealth funds and pension infrastructure allocations.

This is Tom's preferred outcome. Build the network. Operate it. Let it compound.

Path B
Strategic sale

Once a campus has an operating track record — demonstrated utilization, anchor tenants, stable cash flow — it becomes an attractive acquisition target for a Tier 1 hyperscaler (Google, Microsoft, Amazon) looking to expand secondary market presence without the development risk.

The acquirer gets an operating facility with proven demand, established client relationships, and a power cost structure they cannot replicate on a greenfield site. Dominion gets an exit at a valuation reflecting operating infrastructure, not development-stage risk.

A single 48 MW campus at maturity carries an estimated valuation of ~$4.7B at a 12× EBITDA multiple.

Path C Most distinctive
Community transfer

In markets where a community institution — a B Corp, economic development authority, or community foundation — has the will and the financing to acquire the campus, Dominion facilitates a structured installment sale. Title transfers at closing. The community pays from operating revenue over 15–25 years.

USDA Community Facilities program financing covers 40–60% of the acquisition price in qualifying markets, making the purchase viable without institutional capital. The community B Corp that acquires the campus is governed locally — not by Dominion, not by outside investors.

This path produces infrastructure that is permanently anchored in the community it serves. No one else is building this path at scale.

What gets built

Four components.
One integrated facility.

The same facility supports all three paths. The campus components are not co-located by coincidence — they share power infrastructure, networking, security, and staffing in ways that reduce cost and increase resilience for each one.

01
The data center
A 6–25 MW AI compute facility built to Dominion's standard: NVIDIA and AMD GPU infrastructure, on-site natural gas generation at $0.02/kWh, three-tier storage, and a managed Kubernetes control plane. Built to the same technical standard as the Frontier AI Campus — not a cut-down version.
02
The training center
5,000–10,000 sq ft of learning space for workforce training — corporate contracts, public workshops, and GPU lab sessions. The training center uses the same compute cluster the enterprise clients do, with a sandboxed partition for educational workloads.
03
The fiber hub
A regional fiber interconnection point serving local businesses, anchor institutions, and the campus itself. Qualifies for USDA ReConnect and NTIA BEAD program funding. Fiber revenue is recession-proof — anchored by utility contracts and government broadband programs.
04
Co-working space
Flexible workspace for the campus operating team, consulting partners, and local businesses that benefit from physical proximity to AI infrastructure. Generates supplemental revenue and keeps the campus activated during off-peak compute hours.

Path C — Community transfer

How it works.

For markets where community ownership is the right outcome, Dominion operates the campus for 24–36 months, builds the operating track record, then facilitates the sale to a locally governed B Corp. The community ownership structure also unlocks federal financing programs that a purely commercial operator cannot access.

1
Market qualification
Dominion screens for natural gas access, available land, employer demand, and civic institutional will. A community sponsor — chamber, economic development authority, or community foundation — co-sponsors the B Corp formation.
2
Campus build and operation
Dominion develops and operates the campus using Dominion's own capital. The operating period — typically 24–36 months — establishes the track record that makes the USDA financing application credible.
3
USDA pre-application
The Community Facilities pre-application is filed in Month 4 of Year 1 — 12–18 months before the transfer — so the financing closes on schedule. Covers 40–60% of the acquisition price.
4
Community share offering
Community members subscribe for $500–$1,000 as civic memberships — not financial investments. Converts the community into institutional stakeholders before the campus opens.
5
Installment sale closing
Title transfers to the B Corp at closing. The community pays from operating revenue over 15–25 years. Dominion is out of the ownership structure — the campus is governed locally from that point forward.

Federal financing programs

The community ownership structure unlocks capital instruments unavailable to commercial data center developers. The integrated campus — fiber hub, training center, and compute — makes every one of these programs applicable.

USDA Community Facilities
Direct loans and grants for essential community facilities. The fiber hub and training center anchor the application. Covers 40–60% of campus acquisition price.
USDA ReConnect
Grants and loans for broadband infrastructure in rural and underserved areas. The campus fiber hub is a direct fit. Up to $25M per project.
NTIA BEAD Program
$42.5B federal broadband program. State allocations available for qualifying infrastructure projects. The fiber hub positions the campus as eligible infrastructure.
New Markets Tax Credits
Federal tax credit program for investments in low-income communities. Reduces effective cost of capital for the B Corp acquisition by 15–25%.
CDFI Financing
Community Development Financial Institutions provide mission-aligned debt for community-owned infrastructure. Competitive rates, patient capital, aligned incentives.
EDA Grants
Economic Development Administration grants targeting workforce displacement and industrial transition. Applicable in secondary markets with documented manufacturing decline.
