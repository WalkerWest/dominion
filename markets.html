Market strategy

Three sites.
One infrastructure platform.

Dominion is building a multi-site AI compute network along interstate natural gas transmission corridors — where power is cheap, land is available, and no hyperscaler has preceded us. The business does not become three data centers. It becomes one platform operating across three sites.

The three sites

Indiana. Mississippi. Nevada.

Each site adds distinct geographic coverage and sits on a different segment of the US interstate gas transmission network. Together they cover the Midwest, the Gulf Coast, and the Southwest — three separate enterprise markets, one standardized platform.

Site 1 Active
Warrick County, IN
Southwestern Indiana, adjacent to Evansville. Sited on the Midcontinent interstate gas transmission corridor. Covers the Midwest enterprise market — manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and financial services from Chicago to Nashville.
Site 2 Planned
Mississippi
Gulf Coast corridor — Transco, Southern Natural Gas, and Tennessee Gas Pipeline. Mature industrial construction ecosystem from decades of petrochemical and LNG project experience. Covers the Gulf Coast enterprise market: energy, logistics, and manufacturing.
Site 3 Planned
Reno, NV
El Paso Natural Gas and Panhandle Eastern corridor. Pacific time zone coverage for latency-sensitive West Coast clients — without California's permitting complexity, utility interconnect timelines, or power pricing volatility. Completes the continental US footprint.

Site selection

The same four criteria.
Every site.

Every Dominion site is evaluated against the same framework — the same criteria that governed the selection of Warrick County, Indiana for Site 1. Standardization is the discipline that makes the multi-site platform replicable.

Interstate gas transmission corridor proximity
More specific than "access to natural gas." Locations near active compressor stations and maintenance corridors inherit proven supply reliability, established contractor relationships, and lower connection costs. Permitting moves faster — pipeline operators in active maintenance corridors have established regulatory relationships that benefit co-located facilities.
Power cost below $0.05/kWh all-in
The competitive moat depends on maintaining the power cost advantage across every site. Grid-dependent locations at $0.07–$0.12/kWh are not candidates regardless of other advantages.
Favorable permitting environment
States with track records of expedited large-scale industrial permitting and pro-energy regulatory posture. This affects both construction timelines and expansion flexibility within the site.
Distinct enterprise market coverage
Each site must add meaningful new market access. Sites that merely duplicate coverage of an existing location add capacity without adding strategic reach. The three-site footprint was designed to cover three separate geographic enterprise markets.

Why secondary markets

The advantages are structural.
Not temporary.

Secondary market advantages do not erode quickly. They are features of geography, energy infrastructure, and competitive dynamics that compound over time.

Power cost
On-site natural gas generation at $0.02/kWh. A physical asset tied to the site. The advantage doesn't close — it compounds with every megawatt that comes online.
No incumbent competition
In our target markets, Dominion is typically the first hyperscale AI compute provider. That means pricing power, anchor client relationships, and no race to the bottom on margin.
Real regional demand
Regional manufacturers, hospital systems, logistics companies, universities, and government agencies are paying hyperscale cloud rates for sustained inference that would be far cheaper on dedicated infrastructure.
Land and permitting
Available industrial land at rational prices. Local governments want economic development. Permitting timelines that would take years in Northern Virginia take months in our target markets.
Workforce availability
Experienced data center and industrial workforce talent not competing with every tech company in the country. We recruit and retain at costs that reflect the local market.
First-mover federation advantage
The first Dominion campus in a regional network establishes client relationships and operational playbook for every subsequent campus. Being first compounds — each new campus makes the existing network more valuable.
