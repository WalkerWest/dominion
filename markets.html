Secondary market strategy

Where nobody
else is building.

The advantages of secondary markets are structural, not temporary. Power, land, permitting, workforce, and competitive dynamics all favor Dominion's target geography for the foreseeable future.

Why secondary markets

Six structural advantages.
All durable.

Secondary market advantages are not temporary arbitrage. They are structural features of geography, energy infrastructure, and competitive dynamics that do not erode quickly.

Power cost
On-site natural gas generation at $0.02/kWh is the most significant structural cost advantage in AI infrastructure. It does not depend on grid capacity, regulatory approval timelines, or utility relationships. We own the generation.
No incumbent competition
In Tier 1 markets, the competitive dynamic is brutal. In our target markets, we are typically the first hyperscale AI compute provider. That means pricing power, anchor client relationships, and no race to the bottom on margin.
Real regional demand
Regional manufacturers, hospital systems, logistics companies, universities, and government agencies all need AI compute. They are currently paying hyperscale cloud rates for sustained inference that would be far cheaper on dedicated infrastructure.
Land and permitting
Secondary markets have available industrial land at rational prices. Local governments want economic development. Permitting timelines that would take years in Northern Virginia take months in our target markets.
Workforce availability
Secondary markets have experienced data center and industrial workforce talent that is not competing with every tech company in the country for salary. We can recruit and retain at costs that reflect the local market.
First-mover federation advantage
The first Dominion campus in a regional network establishes the technical standards, the client relationships, and the operational playbook for every subsequent campus. Being first compounds — each new campus makes the existing network more valuable.
