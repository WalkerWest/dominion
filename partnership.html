The partnership

Two companies.
One team.
Designed to work together.

Dominion Hyperscale and Merlyn AI Labs were built by the same founding team and are designed to operate as a system. They are legally separate, serve different audiences, and raise different kinds of capital. But they are not independent — they are two halves of a complete AI infrastructure and services platform.

+

What each company is

Different businesses.
Deliberately designed that way.

The separation is intentional. Dominion's capital structure and Merlyn's B Corp mission require different legal homes. The two companies serve different audiences and attract different investors. But they were designed to operate as a system from the start.

Infrastructure developer & operator
Builds and operates the AI data centers.

Dominion identifies sites, raises infrastructure capital, constructs campuses, and operates them. Revenue comes from selling managed compute services — GPU-hours, reserved clusters, dedicated infrastructure — to enterprise clients.

Dominion's structural advantage is $0.02/kWh on-site natural gas generation — a physical asset tied to the site that competitors cannot replicate without the same pipeline access.

Dominion targets institutional capital — infrastructure funds, family offices, sovereign wealth. The business model produces 65–79% EBITDA margins at scale.

  • Does not provide consulting services
  • Does not manage client IT operations
  • Does not run workforce training programs
AI services company & anchor tenant
Delivers AI services to regional employers.

Merlyn operates three service tiers: L1 managed IT and AI literacy, L2 API-level integration and custom model deployment, and L3 financial and operational digital twins. Revenue comes from MSP contracts, consulting engagements, and SaaS subscriptions.

Merlyn runs on Dominion infrastructure as the anchor tenant — its compute spend provides base-load demand certainty for Dominion's Evansville campus from day one.

Merlyn targets community capital — Reg CF community shares at $500–$1,000, USDA financing, and mission-aligned investors. It is structured as a Certified B Corp under steward ownership.

  • Does not build or own data center infrastructure
  • Does not raise infrastructure capital
  • Does not sell raw GPU compute

Where the line is

Who owns what.
Who does what.

The handoffs between the two companies are deliberate and clean. A client relationship that starts with Merlyn can graduate to Dominion without confusion about who is responsible for what.

Dominion owns and operates

The physical facility — land, building, power infrastructure, cooling, networking
The compute cluster — NVIDIA and AMD GPU infrastructure, storage, Kubernetes control plane
The enterprise compute contracts — inference cloud, reserved clusters, dedicated infrastructure tiers
The fiber hub — regional interconnection point, USDA ReConnect and NTIA BEAD eligible
The campus operator relationships — Merlyn leases space and compute from Dominion

Merlyn owns and operates

Client relationships — every regional employer, manufacturer, hospital, and university in the Merlyn client base
The service delivery — managed IT, AI consulting engagements, Academy training programs
The platform software — TeamIQ, Academy LMS, SaaS subscriptions
The workforce pipeline — Academy graduates, corporate training cohorts, certification programs
The community governance — B Corp structure, steward ownership, community share offering

The pipeline

How Merlyn fills
Dominion's facility.

Merlyn's client relationships are the sales pipeline for Dominion's compute business. Every managed IT client is a future compute client. Every Academy cohort is a future self-service infrastructure user. The handoffs are deliberate — and they compound.

1
Merlyn — L1
Managed IT engagement begins
A regional manufacturer, hospital system, or logistics company signs an L1 managed IT contract with Merlyn. Merlyn handles their IT operations, runs AI literacy training for their staff, and begins assessing their AI readiness. Dominion is in the background — the client isn't buying compute yet.
Merlyn revenue
MSP monthly retainer. SaaS subscriptions. Academy training contracts. Non-cyclical — doesn't depend on AI investment trends.
2
Merlyn — L2
First model deployed on Dominion infrastructure
The client is ready to deploy AI into their operations. Merlyn designs and builds the model. It runs on Dominion's managed GPU infrastructure — the client doesn't think about the data center; Merlyn manages that relationship. Dominion now has a paying enterprise compute client introduced by Merlyn.
Both companies earn
Merlyn: consulting engagement fees. Dominion: inference cloud or reserved cluster revenue. The client pays both — for different things.
3
Merlyn — L3
Digital twin deployed at scale
For more sophisticated clients, Merlyn builds financial and operational digital twins — simulation models that run continuously on Dominion's infrastructure. These are high-utilization, sustained workloads. Exactly the kind of compute that fills a data center and justifies the next phase build.
Sustained infrastructure demand
Digital twins run 24/7. High utilization. Not speculative inference — continuous operational workloads that don't go away when the hype cycle turns.
4
Dominion — Direct
Client graduates to self-managed infrastructure
When a client's internal ML team is mature enough to manage their own infrastructure, they graduate from Merlyn-managed L2 to a direct Dominion Compute contract. Same infrastructure — different operating model. Merlyn still holds the consulting relationship; Dominion now holds the compute contract directly.
Dominion direct revenue
Reserved cluster or dedicated infrastructure contract. Higher margin. Long-term committed revenue that supports the next phase capital raise.
5
Dominion — Todd Mosby leads
Enterprise sales runs parallel
Dominion's sales motion doesn't wait for Merlyn introductions. Todd makes direct contact with regional manufacturers, hospital systems, logistics companies, and economic development authorities. The Merlyn pipeline supplements — and validates — the direct enterprise sales motion. Reference clients from Merlyn's book give Todd a warmer door to open.
Enterprise direct pipeline
Dedicated infrastructure contracts and reserved cluster commitments. These contracts trigger the next phase build — not Merlyn's volume, the enterprise accounts.

Why it works

Four structural advantages
that come from the relationship.

Neither company would be as strong without the other. The relationship produces advantages that neither could create independently.

For Dominion
Day-one base-load demand certainty.

A data center with no clients on day one is a liability. Merlyn's managed IT and consulting workloads provide base-load compute demand before a single external enterprise client is signed. That's not a small thing — it means Dominion's break-even utilization is reachable from day one of operation.

And because Merlyn's revenue comes from services regional employers need regardless of AI investment trends, that base-load demand doesn't evaporate when the hype cycle turns.

For Merlyn
Infrastructure economics no independent MSP can match.

An independent managed services company trying to offer AI infrastructure to regional clients has two options: rent from AWS at $0.10/kWh equivalent, or build their own facility — a capital commitment well beyond their means.

Merlyn has a third option. At $0.02/kWh, Merlyn can offer regional clients compute pricing that no independent MSP can compete with. That's not a temporary advantage — it's a physical asset tied to the site. It compounds with every enterprise client Merlyn brings to the campus.

For Dominion
A credible operating partner that opens enterprise doors.

Todd makes direct enterprise sales calls. But a new data center company in a secondary market asking a regional hospital to sign a $2M/yr compute contract is a hard sell without reference clients. Merlyn's existing relationships with regional employers — and the live workloads already running on Dominion infrastructure — give Todd something to point to.

Merlyn converts the campus from a construction project into an operating facility before the first enterprise contract is signed.

For Merlyn
The same team accountable for both layers.

When Merlyn deploys a digital twin for a regional manufacturer, the model runs on infrastructure built and operated by the same people who designed the service. There's no vendor relationship to manage, no SLA dispute to escalate, no infrastructure team that doesn't understand what the workload needs.

That's not a soft advantage. When a production model fails at 2am, the person who picks up the phone knows both the code and the cluster.
